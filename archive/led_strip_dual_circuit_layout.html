Assumption I'm making, please double-check: I read "20 pins on one side, 12 on the other" as the ESP32 module's two physical header rows, and split the design so Circuit A's six signals come from the top (20-pin) row and Circuit B's six come from the bottom (12-pin) row. That's a clean split since Circuit B only needs 6 GPIOs plus the shared GND, but the 12-pin row on most ESP32 dev boards includes non-GPIO pins (EN, 3V3, sometimes only a handful of true usable GPIOs, and a few are input-only or strapping pins). Before wiring this up, pull the pinout diagram for your specific board and confirm the 12-pin side actually has 6 GPIOs safe to use as general-purpose outputs — if it's short a pin or two, some signals may need to be pulled from the 20-pin side instead, which just means adjusting which physical row a couple of the D4–C6 wires start from.
Power now enters the board at a single 2-pin JST connector on the left edge instead of two separate PSU connections. From there, +5V and GND trunk wires branch both up (to feed Circuit A's rails) and down (to feed Circuit B's rails) — so all four rails are really just one +5V net and one GND net, fed from one external plug. Both GND buses are the same electrical net (tied by the trunk), so the single ESP32 GND connection shown near Circuit A is enough for the whole board — no separate ESP32 GND tie-in needed for Circuit B.
