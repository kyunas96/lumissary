Alternate Layout: Power/Ground Flanking the Connector

P taps straight up to the +5V rail, G taps straight down to the GND rail — zero-length, dead simple. The cost: Data and Clock now sit sandwiched between them and have to hop the GND rail to reach the ESP32.
+5V — top rail
GND — bottom rail (in the way)
Data — must hop
Clock — must hop
Worth knowing before you commit to this one: flanking P and G on the outside of each connector does make the power wiring about as simple as it can get — both land directly on their bus with a short, direct stub, no routing decisions to make. But because Data and Clock are sandwiched in the middle, they're now boxed in — the GND bus sits physically between them and the ESP32, so every single one of the 6 data/clock wires has to hop over it. That's double the number of hops compared to the original layout (where only the 3 P-jumpers needed to hop), and hops are exactly the kind of thing that gets fragile if the board gets bumped or flexed later.
If you like this pin arrangement but want to avoid the hops, the front/back trick still applies here: put the GND bus and its 3 short G-jumpers entirely on the back of the board, and run the 6 Data/Clock lines on the front instead (the +5V bus and P-jumpers can live on either face, since P never conflicts with anything). Same pin order, same "flanking" look, zero hops.
