Avoiding the Arch: +5V Wired on the Opposite Face

Instead of arching the red +5V jumpers over the black GND bus, put each network on its own face of the board entirely. They never occupy the same plane, so there's nothing to physically clear.
+5V — lives only on the front
GND — lives only on the back
Data — back
Clock — back
Each hole in the board is a single physical via that both faces share — a wire soldered to it on the front and a different wire soldered to it on the back are still the same electrical node, they just approach from opposite directions. Because the entire +5V network stays on the front and the GND/Data/Clock networks stay on the back, the red and black lines never share a plane at all — not even the small arch from before. No heat-shrink, no glue dot, no clearance to maintain. The only thing to double check per connector: the P pin's lead needs to actually be soldered on the front side pad, while G/D/C get soldered on the back as usual.

The extra black wire routed from the ESP32's own GND pin into the GND bus is what makes this a true common ground — it's the same bus feeding all three LED strips' ground pins, so the ESP32's data/clock signals and the strips' ground reference are identical. This tie-in only needs to happen at one point on the bus; it doesn't need to be repeated per strip, since the bus wire itself is already electrically one continuous node end to end.
