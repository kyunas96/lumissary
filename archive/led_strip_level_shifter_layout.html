ESP32 → 74AHCT541 → 3× LED Strip (Level-Shifted Data/Clock)

ESP32's 3.3V Data/Clock outputs feed the 74AHCT541's inputs. The chip runs on 5V, so its outputs swing full 5V logic to the strips. Power/Ground for the JST connectors still flank as before; the buffer sits in the signal path only.
+5V — bus + chip VCC
GND — bus + chip GND/OE
Data — 3.3V in, 5V out
Clock — 3.3V in, 5V out
How the 74AHCT541 fits in: it's an octal (8-channel) non-inverting buffer, so all 6 of your Data/Clock lines fit on a single chip with 2 channels to spare. ESP32's raw 3.3V outputs go into A1–A6; the corresponding Y1–Y6 outputs come out at whatever you feed VCC (5V here), giving the strips clean 5V logic instead of marginal 3.3V. Both /OE1 and /OE2 are active-low output-enables — tying them straight to GND (as shown) keeps the outputs permanently enabled, so you don't need to manage them from a GPIO. VCC ties to the +5V bus (hopping the GND bus once to get there) and GND ties directly to the GND bus, right next to it — no hop needed since nothing sits between them.
Double-check the real DIP-20 pinout before soldering — A1–A8 and Y1–Y8 are on opposite, mirrored sides of the physical chip (pins 2–9 and 18–11 respectively), with /OE1 at pin 1, /OE2 at pin 19, GND at pin 10, and VCC at pin 20. This diagram simplifies the pin arrangement for clarity — verify against the datasheet when you lay out the actual board. Also add a 0.1µF decoupling capacitor directly across the chip's VCC and GND pins, as close to the chip as possible.
