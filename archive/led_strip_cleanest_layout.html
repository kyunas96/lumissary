Cleanest Layout: Zero Hops, Front/Back Split, 6 Strips + Level Shifting

Same functionality as the flanking design — 6 strips, 2× SN74HCT245, dual ESP32 headers, XT30 power inlet sized for ~12.7A worst case — but back to the original P-G-D-C pin order, split across both faces. No hops, no arches, anywhere.
+5V — front face only
GND — back face only
Data — back face
Clock — back face
220Ω resistor
Why this is cleaner: going back to P-G-D-C pin order instead of flanking means Data and Clock are never sandwiched between two rails — they just drop straight from the JST connector to the chip, and straight from the chip to the ESP32, on one continuous face. GND only ever needs a short tap from G. The only thing that ever needed to "cross" anything was +5V — and that's solved once, structurally, by keeping the entire +5V network (P pins, chip VCC, ESP32 power) on the front face and everything else on the back. Net result: zero hops, zero arches, zero heat-shrink anywhere on either circuit. Each Data/Clock fan wire jogs at its own dedicated row between the connectors and the chip, so no two signal wires ever share a segment.
Capacitors added: each SN74HCT245 now has a 0.1µF decoupling cap right next to it, bridging its VCC via and local GND — small footprint, short leads, sitting as close to the chip's power pins as the layout allows. A 1000µF+ bulk capacitor sits directly at the power inlet, bridging +5V and GND right where power enters the board. Like the other components that need both nets, the bulk cap's +5V lead lives on the front face and its GND lead lives on the back — same via trick used throughout this layout, just applied to a passive component instead of an IC.
Resistors added: a 220Ω series resistor now sits on all 12 Data/Clock lines (6 per circuit), placed on the level-shifter output side — right after each chip's B-pin, before the long run out to the JST connector — per the earlier discussion on damping fast switching edges for RF hygiene near the UWB antenna. Nothing was added on the ESP32-to-chip input side, since that trace is short and doesn't need it.
Spacing fix: the original layout had an asymmetry — Circuit A's top gap (GND bus to JST row) was 121px, but Circuit B's equivalent bottom gap had shrunk to just 11px over successive edits. Both panels have been re-extended so every corresponding gap on Circuit A and Circuit B now matches exactly, keeping the whole layout visually symmetric top-to-bottom around the ESP32.
Chip swap — SN74HCT245 instead of 74AHCT541: same HCT family (TTL-compatible, 3.3V-safe inputs), same DIP-20 package, same 8-channel count, so it drops into the same footprint with no resizing. The real difference: the '245 is a bidirectional transceiver, not a one-way buffer — it has a single OE pin (tied to GND, always enabled, same as before) plus a DIR pin that picks which way data flows. DIR is strapped permanently to VCC (dashed line, routed through the same via as the chip's VCC pin) so the chip always drives A→B — functionally identical to the old '541's fixed one-way behavior, just with one extra strap to wire per chip.
Build checklist (not drawn, but still required): tie each chip's unused inputs (A7, A8) to GND — floating CMOS inputs can oscillate and waste power (unused outputs B7/B8 can be left unconnected). Power inlet and bus wiring are now sized for 6× 1ft, 144 LED/m DotStar strips at an 80%-capped brightness: ~2.1A per strip, ~12.7A combined worst case. The XT30 inlet and 12-14 AWG bus/trunk wiring shown are sized for that combined figure — but the wires from each JST connector out to its own strip only carry that one strip's ~2.1A, so they don't need to match the heavier bus gauge; normal 20-22 AWG is fine there. Worth double-checking your specific strips' own onboard JST-SM connectors too — 2.1A is close to what small JST-SM pins are typically rated for, so confirm your strip's spec rather than assuming headroom.
Same functionality as before: 6 strips, two SN74HCT245 level-shifting transceivers, both ESP32 header rows in use, one shared +5V/GND net, single XT30 power inlet sized for the real current budget. The only real cost of this layout is that a few components (JST connectors, both chips, the ESP32, the power inlet) need leads soldered on both faces at the same holes — a bit more soldering per joint, but each joint is simpler and there's nothing to arch, insulate, or glue down.
