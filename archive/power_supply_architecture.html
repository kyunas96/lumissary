Power Supply Architecture — Split Load, Isolated ESP32 Rail

One battery, three independently-regulated 5V outputs. Each LED circuit gets its own right-sized UBEC; the ESP32 gets a separate, unshared supply so LED current draw never touches its rail. All grounds tie to one common point.

Why this is the simpler design, not a more complex one: instead of hunting for one exotic 15A+ converter, this uses three small, mainstream, well-documented regulators off a single battery. Each LED-side UBEC only has to cover ~6.35A — solidly inside the most common, cheapest, best-specified UBEC category that exists. The ESP32's small BEC only needs 1-2A, so it's a trivial, off-the-shelf part with zero sourcing difficulty. Nothing shares an output rail — each regulator has its own bulk/decoupling cap right at its output — so LED current draw can never sag or inject noise into the ESP32's supply, and one circuit's converter failing doesn't take down the whole display. The only thing genuinely shared across all three branches is ground, which is required for the Data/Clock signal reference between the ESP32 and both LED circuits to work at all.
