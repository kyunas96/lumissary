Use a 20-pin DIP socket for the SN74HCT245, not direct solder: solder the socket to the board and test the surrounding circuit first (VCC, GND, OE/DIR straps) before inserting the chip. This keeps the chip itself out of the way of iron heat during the rest of the build, and lets it be swapped later without desoldering if a data line is ever damaged.
Bulk capacitor placement: the 1000µF bulk cap sits directly on the LED+5V bus, right after the on-board 5A fuse — this is the node that benefits most from it, since it's what absorbs current transients as LED brightness/patterns change, right where the UBEC's output first reaches the board over stranded wire. The ESP-5V rail doesn't get its own separate bulk cap here — the ESP32 module typically carries its own onboard bulk capacitance already.
Bench-testing caution — power both rails together, not independently: LED+5V and ESP-5V share a common GND but are otherwise electrically separate. In normal operation both come off the same battery/splitter and rise together, so this isn't a concern during real use. But if you ever power just the ESP-5V rail alone on the bench (e.g. via USB, while the LED+5V rail is unpowered), the ESP32 could drive the level shifter's A-inputs while the chip has no VCC — forward-biasing its input clamp diodes. Low risk in practice, but worth keeping both rails powered together during testing rather than probing one in isolation.
Fan port — required on every board, connecting a fan to it is optional: this 2-pin JST connector gets soldered onto the board as a standard part of the build, every time — it's not a "skip if unused" component. What's optional is whether a fan actually gets plugged into it. It taps the LED+5V bus (not the isolated ESP-5V rail — fan motor noise shouldn't be introduced onto the ESP32's clean supply), plus GND. Building it in now means a fan can be added later without reopening the board, if handheld use and thermal testing show one is needed — the connector costs nothing to leave unplugged, but it's much cheaper to include now than to retrofit after the enclosure is assembled. A typical 30mm 5V fan draws ~100-200mA, well within the LED bus's headroom (rated 8A, ~3.75A worst-case load already accounted for).
Three fuses total, each protecting a different segment: a 15A fused XT30 pigtail sits right at the battery's main lead, off-board, before the splitter — this is the only protection on the raw side, and it's a simple inline part (fuse holder built into the pigtail), not a separate board. From there, the splitter and both regulators (UBEC, Small BEC) remain unfused themselves, relying on that one upstream fuse plus their own input protection. Each regulator's already-regulated 5V output then comes back onto the board through its own dedicated KF301 screw terminal (bare wire, no connector needed — the regulator's factory output connector is simply cut off), followed by a second fuse sized to that branch's actual load: 5A on the UBEC branch (~3.75A worst case) and 2A slow-blow (T2AL250V) on the Small BEC branch (~1-2A worst case — slow-blow chosen specifically to ride through WiFi/UWB transmit current spikes without nuisance-tripping). Every segment of the power path — raw feed, LED bus, ESP32 rail — has a fuse protecting it.
Wire gauge — 18AWG only where current is combined, 22AWG everywhere else: 18AWG stranded is used only on segments carrying combined/bus-level current — the raw feed (battery → fused pigtail → splitter, ~5-6A combined) and the on-board LED+5V and GND buses (~3.75A). 22AWG stranded covers every individual branch after that: each JST strip's power leads (~1.25A each), the ESP32/Small BEC branch (~1-2A), the fan port, and all Data/Clock lines (current is negligible there — 22AWG is chosen for physical durability and consistency with the rest of the branch wiring, not current capacity). Stranded (not solid core) throughout, given this is a handheld device — solid core work-hardens and cracks under repeated flexing in a way stranded doesn't.
UBEC and Small BEC are external modules, not board-mounted parts: the parts used here (the Henge 8A UBEC, the SoloGood 3A fixed-5V BEC) are self-contained modules on their own short wire pigtail, the same category of thing as the fuse holder. No raw voltage touches the board at all — the battery, splitter, and both modules' unregulated input side are entirely off-board. The board only ever sees already-regulated 5V, arriving through its own KF301 screw terminal on each branch — chosen over a connector here specifically because it eliminates the need to solder anything onto the regulator's output wire at all; the bare wire goes straight into the terminal and gets clamped down with a screw.
Single board, single UBEC, single level shifter: one 8A UBEC comfortably covers the ~3.75A worst-case LED load with real margin, and one level shifter handles all three strips — there's no need to split the circuit across multiple regulators or chips at this scale. The ESP32 still gets its own separate small BEC, isolating it from LED-side switching noise regardless of how many regulators the LED side uses. Antenna separation is achieved through zone placement on the single board: JSTs, chip, and all the power circuitry cluster at the near edge; the ESP32 and its antenna sit at the far edge, with only low-current control wires (Data/Clock, ESP-5V, ESP GND) making the short trip across.
The near/far-edge gap is a deliberate tradeoff, not a spacer: the gap between the near-edge cluster and the ESP32 is close to the minimum needed for solder access and wire dressing, sized for a compact board rather than padded out as RF insurance. This means near-field coupling from the level shifter and UBEC has less distance to fall off before reaching the antenna, so the other mitigations carry more of that load: a small grounded shield if the enclosure allows it, the resistor-damped signal lines, and testing UWB ranging accuracy at the real build spacing before final assembly.
Front/back split wiring and P-G-D-C pin ordering keep every Data/Clock line crossing-free, and the single board covers the full 3-strip, dual-power-rail, fan-ready design described above.

1. System Summary

  • 1 board, 3 addressable LED strips (Adafruit DotStar/SK9822), 1 level shifter, 1 ESP32 (WiFi + UWB), 1 battery, 2 independent voltage regulators.
  • Two 5V rails, deliberately never electrically tied to each other: LED bus (strips + level shifter) and ESP-5V (ESP32 only, isolated).
  • Single shared ground net across the whole board.
  • All raw-voltage components (battery, fuse, splitter, both regulators) are off-board. The board only ever receives already-regulated 5V, through two on-board KF301 screw terminals.

5. Known Open Items

  • Exact antenna location/keep-out on the physical ESP32 module not yet confirmed against the 10mm datasheet spec
  • Whether the ESP32 board has a usable 5V/VIN pin (vs. USB-only power) not yet confirmed
  • UWB ranging accuracy and WiFi signal quality at real build spacing not yet tested
  • Strip wire-to-pin color mapping not yet confirmed against physical strips
  • Measure actual ESP32 peak current before finalizing the 2A fuse — if real peak draw is closer to 0.5-0.7A, consider a 1A fuse for tighter protection
  • Confirm the battery's balance/charge connector configuration directly against the product listing — USB-integrated-charging packs sometimes don't expose a true external XH-3P balance lead
  • Confirm wire fit in XT30 solder cups where 18AWG is used (rated for it, but worth a physical check on the first joint)
