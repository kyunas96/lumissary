Suggested Enclosure Floorplan — Antenna Keep-Out vs Noisy Digital Section

Top-down view. Goal: maximize distance between the WiFi/UWB antenna and the switching digital circuitry (level shifters, data/clock lines), even inside a tight enclosure.

Noisy zone — single board (power + level shifter)
Capacitor (bulk or decoupling)
How to read this: the ESP32 module sits alone in its own zone, with a keep-out radius around the antenna end where nothing else should be placed — no metal, no ground pour, no other components. The single consolidated board (power section, JSTs, level shifter) sits as far away as the enclosure allows, in its own zone, with an air gap (and optionally a small grounded shield) between the two. Signal wires leaving the noisy zone are routed to cross the buffer perpendicular to the antenna's orientation rather than running parallel and close to it, which minimizes coupling.
Capacitor placement, and why it matters for noise too: the two 0.1µF decoupling caps sit right at each SN74HCT245's VCC/GND pins — besides the usual job of keeping the chip's local supply stable during fast switching, they also help absorb some of the high-frequency switching noise right at the source, before it has a chance to radiate or couple onto the GND bus. The 1000µF+ bulk cap sits as close to the power inlet as possible, which is also — conveniently — the farthest point in the noisy zone from the RF quiet zone, so it's not adding any extra proximity risk by being there.
This is a starting layout, not a guarantee — the actual keep-out distance and antenna orientation depend on your specific ESP32 UWB module's datasheet (check the Makerfabs board's silkscreen/schematic for exact antenna placement). Treat the "buffer zone" width as the one dimension worth fighting for in your enclosure design: even doubling a 1cm gap to 2cm can meaningfully cut near-field coupling, since it falls off roughly with the cube of distance. Test with the real hardware at your planned spacing before finalizing the enclosure — run the LEDs at full brightness/update rate and check UWB ranging accuracy and WiFi signal, since that's cheaper than re-designing after the fact.
